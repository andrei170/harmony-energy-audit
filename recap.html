Not a pitch and not a plan yet. Just where the business is, where you told me you want it, and what actually stands in between.

Every figure here came from you. Nothing is measured by us, and nothing is forecast.

One thing in there is easy to miss

You are turning over roughly $600,000 this year on word of mouth alone. No advertising, no website that works, no tracking of any kind. That is the business running on reputation and nothing else.

That is not a company with a demand problem. It is a company with no way for demand to reach it except by somebody already knowing your name.

I ASKED HOW MANY JOBS YOU COULD TAKE. "Probably 20." A month. I ASKED IF GETTING THERE WOULD BE THE GOAL. "That would be great." ON THE MONEY This year's goal $1,000,000 Your own expectation ~$800,000 You used to run $3.5M - $4M a year I ASKED IF ADDING $200,000 - $400,000 THIS YEAR WOULD MAKE YOU HAPPY. "Sure. Sure." AND WHAT IT IS ACTUALLY FOR "It's not good for my guys, because they're not making money. And it's not good for me. But I'm 73 years old. So I don't care either way."
The honest version of your goal

You were straightforward that the money is not for you. At 73, with three businesses behind you and seven children through college, you said plainly that you do not care either way.

What you said twice is that it is not good for your guys. Twelve people, two decades each, on contracts because there is nothing big coming in. That is the thing this is for.

The Distance Between Those Two Places
THE GAP, IN JOBS Doing now 6 a month Could handle 20 a month Unused capacity ~14 jobs a month THE GAP, IN MONEY Expected year end ~$800,000 Goal $1,000,000 Shortfall ~$200,000 WHAT $200,000 - $400,000 ACTUALLY IS At $80,000 a job (your smallest) 3 to 5 jobs At $250,000 a job (your largest) 1 to 2 jobs Spread over the rest of the year.
This is the number worth holding on to

The target you agreed to is between one and five additional projects. Not a rebuild of the company, not a return to four million, and not something that needs your capacity to grow.

You are already carrying fourteen jobs a month you could take and are not being asked for. Five jobs across a whole year is roughly a third of what you could build in one single month.

Why The Obvious Route Will Not Get You There
YOU HAVE ALREADY TRIED BUYING DEMAND Spent: thousands of dollars on leads Result: ~90% had never asked to be contacted Verdict: "They were all garbage." AND THE REASON IT COULD NOT HAVE WORKED Enquiry form on your website none Click-to-call none Any tracking at all none So there was no way to tell a good lead from a bad one, and nowhere for a good one to land even if it arrived.
This is why we are not selling you leads

You bought demand twice and it was junk both times. A third version of that is not what you need and it is not what is being proposed.

The bucket has to hold water before anyone pours any in. Right now somebody who finds you cannot contact you from your own homepage, and nothing records that they were ever there.

What Actually Closes The Gap
FOUR PLACES THE WORK COULD COME FROM 1. THE ORPHANS 17 firms gone. You already service their customers. You did a Sunbug job this week. 2. THE ROOFS Panels on roofs that should never have had them. Five years on, nobody will touch them. "There's a big market for that." 3. YOUR OWN CUSTOMERS 600-700 of them, on SolarEdge inverters you call unreliable. Already generating service traffic. 4. THE FUNDING ARGUMENT REAP and CDFA. "I think we have more experience than a lot of these guys." 1, 2 and 3 need NO advertising spend and NO grant release. 4 is the biggest and the one Washington controls.
Three of those four are already happening, just not recorded

The orphan work is not a theory. You did a job for a Sunbug customer the week we spoke. The service traffic from SolarEdge failures is arriving now. The roof calls come in and you say yes to them.

None of it is written down, measured, or on your website. So it cannot be forecast, cannot be scheduled into a quiet week, and cannot be grown deliberately.

We could not find a single competitor advertising against any of the three. Granite State and ReVision say nothing about orphaned systems, roof refits, or servicing somebody else's install.

And one competitive fact you gave me that is worth watching

You said ReVision used to do a lot of residential and barely does now, and that you never used to see them in commercial at all — but you have lost a few commercial sales to them.

They are moving toward your end of the market while vacating the other. That is both the threat and the opening: the residential and service customers they are leaving behind are the orphans above.

The Money Picture
WHAT THE WHOLE THING COSTS The bigger website, one-off $2,500 Twelve months of us running the advertising $12,000 -------- A FULL FIRST YEAR $14,500 WHAT ONE JOB RETURNS One commercial job, smallest end $80,000 Your net at the 25% you gave me $20,000 $14,500 against $20,000. The website, and a year of advertising management, costs less than the profit on ONE job at the bottom of your range. At $250,000 it is under a quarter of one job's profit. Ad spend is not in that figure. That is our fees only.
So the whole decision comes down to one question

Across the next twelve months, will this produce one additional commercial job? Not five, not a steady stream. One.

If the answer is yes, everything above has paid for itself and the rest is profit. If it is no, then it was the wrong call and you will be able to see that clearly, because measurement is the first thing built.

That is an unusually forgiving piece of arithmetic, and it is entirely because your ticket is large and your capacity is already paid for.

What we are not claiming

Nothing above is a forecast. We are not telling you how many jobs this produces, because we do not know and neither does anybody else who might tell you otherwise.

You have been sold forecasts before by people who charged you thousands for leads that had never heard of you. We are not running that version.
