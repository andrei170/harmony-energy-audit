Five Campaigns. Three Of Them Are For Appointment Volume And Two Are For Money.

Prepared for George Horrocks · 30 September 2026 · AG1 Consulting

If you want a lot of appointments, the commercial campaigns are the wrong ones to start with.

A $150,000 commercial project is searched for rarely, considered for months, and decided by a committee. A failed inverter is searched for today, by one person, who wants somebody out this week.

Both are worth running. They are not the same instrument, and mixing them up is how people conclude that advertising does not work for them.

The three on the left are the unusual part

We could not find Granite State, ReVision or anybody else advertising to customers of dead installers, or offering to take panels off for a roof replacement. That is rare. Most trades have nothing left that nobody is bidding on.

They are also the three that do not compete with your commercial capacity. A service call and a $150,000 install are not drawn from the same pool of hours, so volume here does not crowd out the work you actually want.

Campaign 1 — Orphaned Systems
WHO IS SEARCHING Somebody whose installer has closed. The warranty contact is a dead number. Nobody local will touch another firm's work. WHAT THEY ARE LIKELY TYPING "solar installer went out of business" "who can service my solar panels [town]" "[installer name] out of business solar" "solar panel repair near me" "no one will fix my solar panels" WHERE IT LANDS the orphan-system page (Phase 1 Complete) WHAT SUCCESS IS a booked service visit WHY IT IS CHEAP no competitor found bidding on this intent
The sharpest version of this targets the dead firms by name

You named Sunbug, Sunray and Coastal Solar, and said seventeen in total. Somebody searching the name of a company that no longer exists is the highest-intent search in this entire document, and there is nobody to outbid.

We would want the full list of seventeen from you, because each one is its own small campaign and each one has a known customer base sitting behind it.

Campaign 2 — Roof Removal And Refit
WHO IS SEARCHING A homeowner who needs a new roof and has an array in the way. Their roofer will not touch it. Their installer has gone. They have usually been stuck for weeks. WHAT THEY ARE LIKELY TYPING "remove solar panels for roof replacement" "roofer won't work around solar panels" "solar panel removal and reinstall cost" "need new roof have solar panels" WHERE IT LANDS the roof removal-and-refit page (Phase 1 Complete) WHAT SUCCESS IS a booked survey WHY IT IS CHEAP we found nobody advertising this at all
This is the one we would test first

The person searching it has a problem two trades keep refusing. They are not comparing three quotes on price, they are looking for anybody who will do it, which is the easiest kind of appointment to book.

It also answers itself on the phone. Either you take the roof on or you coordinate with a roofer, and saying which one plainly is the entire offer.

We still need your answer on the roof question

Do you do the roof yourselves, or only the array? The campaign is written differently for each and we cannot run it until that is settled.

Campaign 3 — Inverter Service
WHO IS SEARCHING Somebody whose system has stopped generating, or whose monitoring app has gone dark. Usually urgent, because every day is lost production. WHAT THEY ARE LIKELY TYPING "solar inverter not working" "SolarEdge inverter error [code]" "solar system not producing" "inverter replacement cost" "solar monitoring not working" WHERE IT LANDS the inverter service page (Phase 1 Complete) WHAT SUCCESS IS a booked diagnosis AND: 600-700 of your own customers are on this platform and can be reached by email for nothing
Run the email to your own list before you spend a dollar here

You already hold six to seven hundred customers on a platform you described as unreliable. Writing to people who have already paid you once costs nothing and will tell you how much appetite there is before any budget is committed.

If that email produces bookings, the paid version is worth running. If it does not, you have learned something for free rather than for money.

Campaign 4 — REAP And Commercial Funding
WHO IS SEARCHING A rural business owner or farm operator wondering whether there is money available. Early, researching, not ready to buy. WHAT THEY ARE LIKELY TYPING "REAP grant solar [state]" "USDA grant solar farm" "solar grants for small business [state]" "rural energy grant application help" WHERE IT LANDS the REAP funding page (Phase 1 Essential) WHAT SUCCESS IS a free eligibility check booked WHY IT WORKS no competitor in the set mentions funding at all
Two cautions on this one, and they matter

The offer is the eligibility check, not the grant. You cannot promise funding, the programme has been unpredictable, and you are personally sitting on $2 million of it withheld since late 2024. The advert has to sell a free honest answer rather than money.

And it is slow. Somebody researching grants in September is not installing in October. This is the campaign that fills next year's pipeline, not next month's diary.

Campaign 5 — Commercial Solar, By Sector
WHO IS SEARCHING A farm, packhouse, workshop or small manufacturer with a large roof and a daytime load. WHAT THEY ARE LIKELY TYPING "commercial solar [state]" "solar for farms [state]" "solar panels for warehouse" "commercial solar installer near me" WHERE IT LANDS the commercial page (Phase 1 Complete) WHAT SUCCESS IS a site assessment booked WHY IT IS THE DEAREST everybody sells installation, so you are bidding against the market
Worth running, worth running last

This is the campaign with your $80,000 to $250,000 jobs in it, so it is the one that closes the $400,000 gap to your goal. It is also the most expensive attention on the page, because it is the only one where you are competing with every other installer.

Run it once the cheaper three have paid for themselves, and run it split by sector rather than as one campaign, because a farm and a workshop are not persuaded by the same thing.

The Order We Would Run Them In
  • Nothing at all until Phase 1 is live. There is currently no form, no click-to-call and no conversion tracking. Sending paid traffic to that loses everybody who was not ready to phone, and nobody could tell you whether it worked.
  • The free email to your own 600-700 first. Costs nothing, reaches people who have already bought, and tells you whether the service appetite is real before any budget.
  • Then roof refit and orphan systems. Cheapest attention, most urgent searcher, quickest to a booked appointment. These are your volume.
  • Then inverter service as paid, once the free email has shown what the demand looks like.
  • Then REAP, knowing it is slow, because it builds a pipeline rather than a diary.
  • Commercial by sector last, funded by what the others produced.
What we are deliberately not telling you

We have not run keyword research yet, so there are no search volumes, no cost-per-click estimates and no appointment forecasts anywhere on this page. The search terms above are hypotheses about how people describe these problems, not measured data.

The first job in any of these campaigns is the keyword report, which tells us which of the five is actually worth the money. Anybody quoting you a cost per appointment before that has made it up.

Three campaigns nobody is bidding against, and two where everybody is.

Start with the ones nobody wants and use the money to buy the ones everybody does.
