Finding 1: A Correction. The Roof Refits Have A Competitor In Your County
What we said in September, and what is true now

In September we wrote that the orphan systems were “the only finding on the page with no competitor in it”. We had measured homepages only. Reading the service pages this time:

  • New England Clean Energy has a page called “Solar Panel Removal and Reinstallation”, headed “A New Roof Shouldn’t Mean Starting Over With Solar”.
  • Its service area on that page includes Rockingham County, New Hampshire and York County, Maine.
  • Its repair page says it “reviews service requests for qualifying installations completed by other companies”, and that support “depends on equipment”.

So the idea was right, and somebody else has seen it too. That is a reason to move sooner, not a reason to drop it.

Finding 2: What None Of Them Says
The opening that is still yours

Nobody says plainly “your installer has closed, we will look after your system”. New England Clean Energy's wording is careful and conditional (“can evaluate requests”, “depends on equipment”). Granite State and ReVision say nothing about other firms' systems on the pages we read.

You are already doing this work and you know which firms have gone. A page that speaks directly to those owners says something none of the three is saying.

One thing we noticed, worth asking you about

On ReVision's service page, the map link for its Westfield, Massachusetts office opens a Google listing still named “Sunbug Solar”. You told us Sunbug was bought out. We have not checked who bought it, so we are not drawing a conclusion. If Sunbug's customers now sit with ReVision, that changes how the orphan page should talk about them.

Finding 3: They Kept Building. You Stood Still.
Since September

Granite State Solar's homepage grew from 1,832 to 2,642 words. New England Clean Energy is still running a full tracking stack including a Google Ads conversion tag, which means it is spending on ads and measuring them. Your site is the same as it was, including the broken Contact Us link (see Audit Pt.2).

None of that is a reason to panic. It is a reason not to let another two months go by.

Finding 4: The Grant Is Still Nobody Else's
REAP and USDA, rechecked

None of the three mentions REAP or USDA on its homepage, the same as in September. You put funding on hold in October until Washington is clearer, and nothing here changes that order. It does mean the position is still there for you when you want it.

What This Means For Thursday
  • Orphan systems first, as you decided, with a page that says plainly what the others will not.
  • Removal and reinstall second, now with a named competitor on it in your county.
  • Your own customers by email, which no competitor can reach.
  • Fix the Contact Us link whatever you decide.
