Where We Left Off, Before Today.

Prepared for George Horrocks and Jaron · for our call on Thursday 19 November, 2pm Eastern · AG1 Consulting

It has been a few weeks since we spoke, so here is everything on one page: what you told me, what you want, the plan you put together, and what is left to settle.

Nothing here is new. It is your words and your decisions, so we can pick up exactly where we stopped.

Our calls • Where things stand • Your goals • Your plan • Today
Our Three Calls
29 September I went through an audit of your website and three local competitors. You told me how the business got here: from $3.5M to $4M a year down to about $600,000, the USDA money being held, and the 17 installers who have gone and left their customers with nobody. 30 September I brought five campaign ideas and two website mock-ups. You asked about AI search and about editing the site yourself. You leaned towards the bigger website, and wanted Jaron to see it. 12 October You put the plan in order: orphan systems first, removal and reinstall second, an email to your own customers third. You set your area and cut the town pages. You wanted time to think and to talk it over with Jaron.
Where Things Stood, In Your Words
Your crew

“All of my guys right now, who have worked for me for 20 years, are just as contractors now, because we just have no big things coming in.” Twelve people. You said it twice: it is not good for them.

Where the work comes from

Word of mouth and referral, nothing else. About six jobs a month (“we’re lucky if we get half a dozen”) when you could handle around twenty.

Bought leads have not worked: “They were all garbage.” Most of those people had never asked to be contacted.

Your website

No inquiry form, no number people can tap on a phone, and no tracking, so somebody who finds you has no easy way to reach you, and you cannot see where work comes from. Rechecked this week: your Contact Us link now goes to a page that does not exist. The detail is on Audit Pt.2.

The money side

The USDA grants held since late 2024, solar down this year, and you were straight with me that you did not want to go deeper in the hole. That is why we talked about starting small and splitting the setup.

What You Said You Want
Jobs a month about 6 now around 20 "That would be great." This year about $800,000 $1,000,000 The gap 1 to 5 more commercial jobs, at $80,000 to $250,000 each What it is for your crew back on steady work
The Plan You Put Together On The 12th
⭐ 1. Orphan systems

“A terrible need out there. We get calls all the time, but rather than waiting until people find me, it makes more sense to be advertising about that.”

⭐ 2. Removal and reinstall

When somebody is being re-roofed and the panels have to come off and go back on, and you take over the monitoring. “There’s a big market for that.”

⭐ 3. Your own customers, by email

Inverter service for your 600 to 700 customers. Costs nothing in advertising and keeps the crews busy.

Later

REAP and funding on hold until it is clearer what Washington does. Commercial solar last, once the first three are paying for themselves, with the 30% tax credit ending with 2027 at the top of that page.

Your area: all of New Hampshire, all of Massachusetts, and southern Maine south of Portland. No town pages.

What Has Changed Since We Spoke
Someone else has started on your number two

New England Clean Energy now has a “Solar Panel Removal and Reinstallation” page covering Rockingham County NH and York County ME, and says it reviews systems installed by other companies. When we first looked in September, nobody local was doing this. The detail is on Competitors Pt.2.

For Today
1. How have the last few weeks been for work, and for the crews? 2. What did Jaron think, and would you both like the site on WordPress? 3. Has anything moved on the USDA money? 4. Yes or no on starting with the orphan-system and removal and reinstall pages. 5. The list of installers that have gone: Sunbug, Sunray, Coastal Solar, Solar Wolf and the rest. The first campaign is built from it.
The cost, as we left it

Nothing has changed since 12 October. Half of the website to start and the other half once the campaigns are bringing you work, your first month of management included, then month to month. The website is yours either way. Line by line on Deliverables & Pricing.
