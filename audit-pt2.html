What You Told Me You Need, And What Your Website Says About It Today.

Prepared for George Horrocks · for our call on Friday 20 November, 2pm Eastern · AG1 Consulting
F
29 / 100, unchanged since 28 September

Nothing on the September scorecard has moved, and the recheck found one problem that matters more than all of it: the “Contact Us” link in your menu goes to a page that does not exist.

The first audit measured the site against the market. This one goes through the things you said you need, in your words, and checks what the site does about each of them today.

Your words • What we found on 18 November • What fixes it
The One To Fix Whatever You Decide
Your “Contact Us” menu link is broken

The menu on every page has a “Contact Us” link. It points to harmonyenergyworks.com/?page_id=1781, and that address returns “page not found” (error 404). We checked it again on 18 November.

Your work comes from word of mouth. Somebody one of your customers sent your way looks you up, clicks “Contact Us”, and lands on an error. Your number is in the header as plain text (603.926.3366), but it is not a tap-to-call link, so on a phone they have to copy it.

Two more links in the menu go nowhere: “Gallery” and “Testimonials” point to an old staging address (ipa.vqa.mybluehost.me) that does not load. The real pages exist at /our-work/gallery/ and /our-work/testimonial/, so this is a link problem, not missing content.

What fixes it

Point the three menu links at the right pages, make the phone number tap-to-call, and add a short inquiry form. On a WordPress site this is an hour or two of work, and it is worth doing this week whether or not we work together.

1. The Orphan Systems
WHAT YOU SAID (12 OCTOBER) "A terrible need out there. We get calls all the time, but rather than waiting until people find me, it makes more sense to be advertising about that."
What we found on 18 November

The words “orphan”, “other installers” or anything about looking after a system somebody else put in appear nowhere on your homepage, Our Work, Solar 101 or Our Team pages. The homepage mentions inverters once, in a sentence about the brands you install.

And this ground is no longer empty. New England Clean Energy now has a repair and maintenance page that says it “reviews service requests for qualifying installations completed by other companies.” See Competitors Pt.2.

What fixes it

A page written to the owner whose installer has gone: we look after systems we did not install, here is what we check, here is how to reach us. It is stronger with the list of firms that have closed or stopped supporting customers, which is why we keep asking for it.

2. Removing And Refitting Panels For A New Roof
WHAT YOU SAID (12 OCTOBER) When somebody is being re-roofed and the panels have to come off and go back on. We take over the monitoring too.
What we found on 18 November

Your homepage does not mention removal, reinstalling, or roofs at all. The only roof content is on Solar 101, about how panels are mounted.

New England Clean Energy has a whole page for exactly this (“A New Roof Shouldn’t Mean Starting Over With Solar”), and it lists Rockingham County, New Hampshire, your county, and York County, Maine as areas it covers.

What fixes it

A removal and reinstall page that roofers can send people to as well as homeowners. The roofer is the one who finds out first that the panels have to come off, so the page should make it easy for a roofing company to book you.

3. Your Own 600 To 700 Customers, By Email
WHAT YOU SAID (12 OCTOBER) Inverter service as an email to your own customers, not an advert. It costs nothing in advertising and keeps the crews busy.
What we found on 18 November

There is no way on the site for an existing customer to ask for a service visit, and nothing that captures an email address. The inner pages carry a WordPress comment box (“Leave a Reply”), which is not an inquiry form and is not where a customer with a fault should end up.

What fixes it

A short “book a service check” page, and the email itself. The email does not need the website to be finished first. It needs your customer list with email addresses, which is a question for Friday.

4. Being Able To Change It Yourselves
WHAT YOU ASKED (30 SEPTEMBER) Whether you and Jaron would be able to edit the site yourselves.
What we found on 18 November

The current site is WordPress, on a theme called “Striking”. The footer says Copyright 2018, and the newest items on the blog are about the 2014 REAP deadline and net metering. The homepage says you are “the only solar company in NH” to win USDA REAP grants for commercial customers in 2011 and 2012. That is a strong line, and it reads as old because nothing newer sits beside it.

What fixes it

Yes, you will be able to edit it, on WordPress if that is what you and Jaron prefer. And one newer funding story next to the 2011-2012 one would make the credential read as current.

Questions That Change What We Build

  • Do you have email addresses for your 600 to 700 customers? That decides when the inverter email can go out.
  • Which number goes on the site: the office line 603.926.3366, or your own 603.512.3377?

  • WordPress or not, and has Jaron had a look?
